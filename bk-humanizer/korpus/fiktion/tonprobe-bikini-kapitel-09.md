# Tonprobe Kapitel 9 — Boutique

**Chapter 9 — Boutique scene**

The boutique opened at nine. At ten to, I was already outside, pulling the hem of the blue sundress down again.

“You’ll stretch it,” Alexa said.

“Good.”

She checked her phone. I let go of the hem, waited a few seconds, and pulled it down again. Yesterday I had spent an afternoon in a bikini and a sarong. Today I intended to buy something I could walk around in without holding on to it.

The girl who unlocked the door had her hair tied back and a name tag that said Pilar. She smiled as we came in.

“Good morning. What are you looking for?”

“Jeans and a T-shirt.”

“We don’t carry jeans. We have shorts.”

She took a pair from the display beside the counter. I held them up. They would cover about as much as the dress I was wearing.

“Something longer.”

“For the beach?”

“For breakfast. For walking to breakfast. Anything past my thighs would be a start.”

“Let me look in the back.”

She disappeared through a curtain. I checked the nearest rack myself, moving aside a crop top to find three more behind it.

“We could get a taxi into Playa,” I said. “There must be somewhere to buy normal clothes.”

“There is. It’s quite a drive from here.”

“How far?”

Pilar came back with a green dress over her arm. “About an hour. Sometimes an hour and a half, with traffic.”

“Each way?”

“Yes.”

I looked down at the blue dress. Two or three hours in a taxi, plus however long it took to find a shop and work out what size I was. I would go if I had to, but I had hoped to have this dealt with before breakfast.

“Try this first,” Pilar said.

She held up the green dress. It had short sleeves and a skirt that looked as though it might reach my knees. I took the hanger from her and turned it around. The back was covered too.

“That’s better.”

“Fitting room’s just there.”

I stepped behind the curtain and hung the dress on the hook.

The blue dress came off easily. Getting the green one on took more work. I nearly got stuck before finding the zipper under the left arm, then had to pull it up over my head again to undo it properly.

“Everything okay?” Alexa asked.

“There’s a zipper.”

“Usually is.”

“I’ve found it. Thank you.”

The second attempt went better. I worked my arms into the sleeves, pulled the dress down and reached for the zipper at my side. Once it was closed, I smoothed the skirt and looked in the mirror.

Oh. That was much better.

The neckline covered the bra I had struggled into that morning, and the sleeves covered my shoulders. The skirt stopped just above my knees. I could stand normally without feeling that I ought to put a hand in front of myself.

I tried sitting on the little stool. The hem rose a few inches, but a quick adjustment kept it where I wanted it. I stood again and reached up as though taking something from a shelf. Nothing slipped. Nothing needed pulling back into place.

I could wear this.

The green brought out the red in my hair. I gathered it at the back of my neck, then let it fall over my shoulders again. Loose was better. The dress fitted neatly at the waist, then fell away over my hips. I turned sideways to check it, then back to the front.

“Well?” Alexa asked.

“It’s long enough.”

“Can I see?”

I checked the neckline once more and opened the curtain.

She looked at me, then down at the hem. I waited for a comment about my knees or how long I had been in there.

“That looks lovely on you,” she said.

“I can sit down in it.”

“That helps.”

“And I don’t have to keep checking the front.”

“You’ve checked it twice since you came out.”

I took my hand away from the neckline.

Alexa reached for a black dress hanging beside the mirror.

“What about a nice evening dress while we’re here?”

“For you?”

“For you.”

“No. Look at it.”

She held it up and turned it around. The back was open almost to the waist.

“There’s no back,” I said. “Half my boobs would be hanging out at the front, and what’s that at the bottom? Six inches of skirt? I couldn’t bend over to pick up my bag without giving the whole restaurant a view. I asked for something to cover me up, Alexa. It’s a sorry excuse for not being naked.”

She was smiling now.

“I’m not wearing it.”

“Let’s see how long this lasts.” She turned to Pilar. “Could you put it aside for us?”

“Of course.”

Pilar took the black dress behind the counter. I went back to the fitting room for the blue one.

“Just the green,” I said when I handed it over. “And I’ll keep it on, if that’s okay.”

“Of course.”

At the counter I reached for my wallet and found the side of the dress. Alexa already had our cards out of her bag.

“Yours or mine?” she asked.

“Yours.”

She used hers.

Pilar folded the blue dress into tissue paper. “Green is lovely with strawberry blonde.”

“Strawberry blonde?”

“Your hair.” She glanced up at me. “It’s a good color for you.”

I looked in the mirror behind her. My hair was still lying loose over the green, the way I had left it in the fitting room.

“It was the length I wanted,” I said.

Pilar nodded and put the tissue into a bag.

Alexa took it, then passed it to me while she put the cards away. At the door I took her tote as well, leaving her to hold the door while I walked out with both bags.

“Do you still want to go into Playa?” she asked.

“Not today. This will do.”

We headed back to the room to put the bags down. At the stairs I handed Alexa her tote and reached for the railing. I had walked all the way across the courtyard without pulling at the hem once.
